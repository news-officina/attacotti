US soldier arrested in England on suspicion of spying for Russia

The 41-year-old was detained near Gloucester under the National Security Act 2023 and remains in custody at a London police station. The FBI director said the case involves allegations of espionage, which can carry a life sentence before an American military court.

11 November 2026

A 41-year-old US Army soldier was arrested near Gloucester on 10 November on suspicion of collecting secret information and helping Russian intelligence. He was arrested on suspicion of offences under Section 1 and Section 3 of the National Security Act 2023, the law used to prosecute spying for a foreign power. The allegations, part of an investigation by Counter Terrorism Policing, the UK police unit for terrorism cases, relate to Russia.

The soldier was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, headquarters of Nato's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, according to the BBC. It came out of an investigation involving US authorities, the US Military and the FBI. Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, of Counter Terrorism Policing London, described the arrest as "significant" and said the allegations are serious. She added that the investigation is very much ongoing. Officers are searching two locations in Gloucestershire.

The FBI director, Kash Patel, issued a statement. It said: "This important arrest involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies." Patel said the FBI has made 167 counterintelligence and espionage arrests over the last 21 months, a 53% increase.

The soldier remains in custody at a London police station, detained under the National Security Act 2023, with Counter Terrorism Policing London leading the investigation. ABC News reported that he could face charges from both British authorities and the US Army. Espionage charges before an American military court can carry sentences up to life imprisonment. Brig Gen Sean Stinchon, commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command, said the case was a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent. Neither British nor US officials released his name.

Detention under the act allows police to hold him without charge for up to 14 days. Counter Terrorism Policing said the investigation is not linked to any other ongoing counterterrorism investigations.
