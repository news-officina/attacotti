Ten JEF countries sign naval partnership pact to deter Russian threats

The British-led agreement commits the navies of Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the UK to interchangeable forces, commanded from Northwood in London. Norway's Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre called it a response to American calls for allies to carry more of the burden.

9 November 2026

Ten countries in the Joint Expeditionary Force, a British-led defence group, will sign an agreement on partnership between their naval forces today, November 10. It is aimed at deterring future threats from Russia in the region. The Norwegian government, which put the plan forward, presents it as a broad maritime partnership. In July, Washington told European allies it would cut by a third the fighter aircraft it would make available to NATO in a crisis. It would halve the bombers and no longer provide submarines.

Norway's Prime Minister, Jonas Gahr Støre, called it a significant contribution to security in the North Atlantic and High North. He called it a direct response to American calls for others to take more responsibility. The First Sea Lord, General Gwyn Jenkins, said the grouping would complement NATO and answer crises faster, and that the joint forces would be commanded from Northwood, the Royal Navy's main maritime headquarters in London. In Moscow, Andrei Denisov, deputy chairman of the Federation Council's foreign affairs committee, called the plan another provocation and escalation. He said the impulse comes from Great Britain, which no longer hides behind small European states.

The ten members are Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom. In July, all ten formally agreed to the Maritime Proposition, which commits their navies to align doctrine, training, planning, sustainment and capability development so that equipment, parts, ammunition or personnel can be substituted, swapped or mixed across the participating forces. On 2 October, defence ministers of the JEF and allied countries met at Keflavik Air Base in Iceland to advance the group's role in European and Euro-Atlantic security.

In July, Jenkins called the agreement a historic change, shifting from periodic cooperation to a truly integrated approach. He said forces could combine at pace and fight immediately together. The JEF defence ministers said in October that their nations seek no confrontation and pose no threat to Russia. Russia, they said, should be in no doubt that they will defend themselves. El País's reporting on alliance sources described the move as burden sharing.

The Royal Navy's involvement is being led by Jenkins, and the week beginning 9 November will see HMS Prince of Wales host the Atlantic Future Forum and the International Sea Power Conference in Oslo, attended by European military and political leaders. In October, Canada applied to join the framework, with defence ministers then working towards a recommendation to JEF leaders.
